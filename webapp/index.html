Intelligence Core

This project is not built around a single model. It is an evolving nephrotoxicity intelligence stack that combines similarity reasoning, descriptor learning, transformers, latent compression, and ensemble decision layers to support safer chemical screening.

Meta-Ensemble Decision Layer

The strongest current engines are ensemble systems that blend KNN, LightGBM, and logistic meta-learning so the predictor can combine local chemical similarity with global decision boundaries.

Chemical Language Models

ChemBERTa-based pipelines treat SMILES as a language, helping the system read structure patterns that are difficult to capture with fingerprints alone.

Latent Representation Models

The autoencoder branch compresses high-dimensional fingerprints into a denser latent space, giving the project an unsupervised view of hidden toxicity structure.

Similarity and Retrieval Logic

Similarity-driven models remain important because kidney-risk prediction is often shaped by structural neighborhoods, scaffold relationships, and chemical proximity.

Model Constellation

This project has explored tree models, GNNs, stacked ensembles, project-plus hybrids, noteworthy experiments, and SOTA-inspired baselines before converging on the current main set.

Human and Clinical Relevance

Nephrotoxicity prediction matters because earlier detection of kidney-risk compounds can support safer drug design, reduce failed candidates, and protect patients from avoidable harm.

Research Heritage

This page tracks the evolution of the predictor across the many combinations tested in the project. It shows how the system moved from isolated experiments toward a more resilient multi-family nephrotoxicity platform.

Phase 01

Tanimoto Gaussian Process

The similarity-first stage focused on chemical distance and probabilistic scaffold reasoning. It established an interpretable baseline and kept structural analogy at the center of the project before the broader prototype families expanded.

Phase 02

Tree and Tabular Acceleration

Modular LightGBM, HistGradientBoosting, and related tabular routes tested whether strong non-linear descriptor learners could outperform more classical baselines while staying efficient and explainable.

Phase 03

Graph Neural Network Exploration

Virtual GIN, modular GIN, and NODE-style experiments pushed the project beyond fixed descriptors, aiming to learn directly from graph structure and internal molecular connectivity.

Phase 04

Project-Plus Hybrids

The project-plus line introduced project-plus GIN, project-plus GIN hybrid, and project-plus ChemBERTa combinations, giving the system a broader search space across graph, sequence, and tabular representations.

Phase 05

Noteworthy and SOTA Tracks

The nephro SOTA line plus noteworthy fixed and noteworthy final tracks acted as stress tests for promising ideas, helping identify what transferred well to external evaluation and what remained brittle.

Phase 06

Stacking and Full-Coverage Systems

Modular stacking ensembles and the full-coverage meta-model families became the turning point: they combined multiple perspectives so the predictor could stay expressive, broad, and more stable on external molecules.

Current Frontier

Five Main Engines, One Safer-Screening Story

The current dashboard centers on five main engines: Full Coverage Ensemble, Descriptor + Fingerprint Ensemble, Autoencoder Model, ChemBERTa Hybrid, and Similarity Model. Together they represent the most understandable and presentation-ready form of the project so far.

Why It Matters

From Model Experiments to Human Impact

Every iteration in this project supports the same goal: help flag nephrotoxicity risk earlier, support medicinal chemistry decisions sooner, and reduce the chance that harmful compounds reach patients or late-stage development.
